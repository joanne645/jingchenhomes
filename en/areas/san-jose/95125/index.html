95125 | Willow Glen: established streets and homes with character

Willow Glen sits southwest of Downtown, roughly between I-280, SR-87 and Los Gatos Creek, and locally the name usually means the older streets on either side of Lincoln Avenue. It is known for established residential streets, homes from many eras, and the shops and restaurants around Lincoln Avenue. Early twentieth-century bungalows, Tudors and Spanish Revival homes stand alongside postwar ranch houses, and that variety between individual homes is the defining feature of this market.

What buyers should know: Within Willow Glen, homes can differ widely in age, lot shape, parking, quality of work and walkability. With older homes, also look at the foundation, drainage, termites, permit history and insurance cost.

What sellers should know: A single price per square foot does not work well here. Historic character, upkeep, outdoor space and proximity to busy streets all need to show up in the pricing analysis.

Willow Glen in depth

Location and boundaries

Willow Glen sits southwest of Downtown, roughly between I-280, SR-87 and Los Gatos Creek, and past Curtner Ave to the south it gradually blends into Cambrian. The name means two things. It is one of the city’s 15 Planning Areas, a fairly large one, large enough that city park data even places Camden Community Center on Union Ave inside it. In everyday local use, though, Willow Glen usually means the older streets on either side of Lincoln Ave in ZIP code 95125. Neither version is a legal boundary or a school attendance area, so go by the specific address.

Homes

Single-family homes dominate. Around Lincoln Ave you’ll find bungalows, Tudors and Spanish Revival homes from the first half of the twentieth century; farther south and west the homes shift to postwar ranches, with plenty of rebuilt or expanded houses mixed in. Older homes here often come with small lots, a detached or one-car garage, and fewer bedrooms and baths than buyers expect today. On these homes, focus your inspection on the foundation, whether wiring and plumbing have been updated, permits for any additions, and what street tree roots are doing to sidewalks and sewer lines.

Daily life

Life here centers on Lincoln Ave, an old commercial street lined with restaurants, coffee shops and neighborhood stores; Willow Glen Elementary is on the same street at 1425 Lincoln Ave. Willow Glen Branch Library is at 1157 Minnesota Ave, and the Willow Glen Community & Senior Center is at 2175 Lincoln Ave. Willow Street Frank Bramhall Park (1320 Willow St) has an amphitheater and reservable picnic areas, and to the south are Wallenberg Park (Curtner Ave at Cottle Ave) and Lincoln Glen Park. The Los Gatos Creek Trail works for biking and running. City trail data shows the west section of the Three Creeks Trail running from Lonus St by way of Coe Ave, Broadway Ave and Willow St to Minnesota Ave, but check the city’s trail page to see which segments are open before you go.

Commute and schools

Most drivers rely on SR-87 and I-280, and surface streets work fine for getting Downtown. Rail is on the area’s eastern edge: Tamien station has both Caltrain and VTA’s Blue Line, with Curtner station farther south, and VTA lists more than 200 parking spaces at Tamien. Try the trip from the Lincoln Ave area to the station yourself, whether you plan to drive, bike or get dropped off. For schools, this area is in San José Unified School District (TK–12), whose list includes Willow Glen Elementary, Booksin, Schallenberger, Willow Glen Middle and Willow Glen High. On March 26, 2026, the district board approved the Schools of Tomorrow plan, closing five elementary schools starting in 2026–27. Canoas Elementary merges into Ernesto Galarza Elementary, and the Hammer Montessori program moves from Galarza to the Gardner site. New attendance boundaries take effect in 2027–28 for new students only, so the same address can return different schools this year and next, and the district provides a separate address lookup for each school year.

The trade-offs

Choosing Willow Glen gets you a commercial street within walking distance and a mature streetscape. In exchange, you take on the upkeep of an older home, smaller garages and storage, and the permits and cost of any remodel. Streets near SR-87, I-280 and the Caltrain tracks get traffic and rail noise, and curb parking near Lincoln Ave tightens on weekends and event days. On the southern streets affected by school mergers, enrollment needs to be confirmed separately for the year a student would start.

What to look for on a visit

Spend a Saturday morning on Lincoln Ave to get a feel for the foot traffic and parking, then come back on a weekday evening and drive from the home you’re considering to the SR-87 on-ramp and to Tamien station. Stand outside the home and listen for planes, trains and freeway noise. When you look at an older home, check whether either neighbor is rebuilding and whether street parking is already full.
