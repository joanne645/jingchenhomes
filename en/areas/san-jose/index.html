Why prices differ by ZIP code

San Jose is not a single real estate market. ZIP codes differ in home types, lots, school boundaries and buyer demand, and understanding those differences matters more than a price ranking.

Median price, sales and days on market for each ZIP code are being verified for a single period and will be published once confirmed.

ZIP code guides

95118 | Cambrian / South San Jose: postwar homes where school districts meet

95118 sits in southwest San Jose between Cambrian and Blossom Valley, with Blossom Hill Rd, Branham Ln and Meridian Ave as the everyday roads and SR-85, SR-87 and Almaden Expressway close by. Most homes are single-story ranches from the 1950s through the 1970s on regular lots, usually with two-car garages, and Blossom Valley adds scattered townhomes from the 1970s and 1980s. Vineland Branch Library is on Blossom Hill Rd, Oakridge to the southeast handles bigger shopping trips, and getting around mostly means driving. What sets this ZIP code apart is that several school districts meet here, so neighborhoods with similar names can fall under different school systems.

What buyers should know: Put schools first on your list. Pioneer High is in San José Unified and Branham High is in Campbell Union HSD, and both campuses are in 95118, while elementary and middle schools here also involve San José Unified, Union School District and others. Confirm the full address with the district rather than relying on a listing. On older ranch homes, look at the original sewer line, single-pane windows, roof, HVAC and permits for additions.

What sellers should know: Group comparable sales by school district first, then by home type and lot. Homes a few blocks apart may belong to different districts, and a 1970s townhome should not sit in the same comparison set as a detached house. Have dates for roof, plumbing and sewer work ready along with permits for additions, and keep school information in the listing to the district and its official lookup.

95120 | Almaden Valley: space, hill views and holding costs

Almaden Valley sits at the south end of San Jose, a valley between the Santa Teresa Hills and the Sierra Azul, near the foothills and open space. Nearly all homes here are single-family, most built from the 1960s to the 1980s, with lots and garages generally roomier than in central neighborhoods. Almaden Lake Park, the Los Alamitos Creek Trail and Almaden Quicksilver County Park are close by, while everyday shopping usually means driving. Buyers tend to focus on home size, yards and the neighborhood setting, and on the commute trade-off to Silicon Valley’s main job centers.

What buyers should know: Check insurance availability, foothill fire risk, drainage, retaining walls, the roof and upkeep costs. Conditions vary a lot by location and property, so the whole ZIP cannot be treated as one risk level.

What sellers should know: Separate standard homes, large-lot properties, view homes and high-end remodels. With few high-end sales, a single month’s median can move simply because of which homes sold.

95124 | Cambrian: classic single-family homes and a workable commute

95124 covers much of the Cambrian area, bordering Campbell and Los Gatos, with SR-85 and SR-17 to the south and west and Camden Ave, Union Ave, Hillsdale Ave and Bascom Ave as the main local roads. These are established low-rise neighborhoods, mostly single-story ranch homes built in tracts in the 1950s and 1960s, typically with three or four bedrooms and two-car garages on regular lots. Cambrian Branch Library, Camden Community Center and Houge Park are spread through the area, Good Samaritan Hospital is on Samaritan Dr, and most errands happen by car. Some parcels are unincorporated county land even though they carry a San Jose mailing address. Buyers usually compare classic single-family homes, yard space, how much updating a home needs, and the commute.

What buyers should know: Look hard at the roof, drainage, plumbing, electrical and foundation, and confirm that any additions were permitted. A new kitchen and floors do not tell you those things. School boundaries inside Cambrian also need checking address by address.

What sellers should know: For classic homes here, a pre-listing inspection, needed repairs, sensible staging and a sharp set of comparable sales usually do more than a general “great schools” pitch.

95125 | Willow Glen: established streets and homes with character

Willow Glen sits southwest of Downtown, roughly between I-280, SR-87 and Los Gatos Creek, and locally the name usually means the older streets on either side of Lincoln Avenue. It is known for established residential streets, homes from many eras, and the shops and restaurants around Lincoln Avenue. Early twentieth-century bungalows, Tudors and Spanish Revival homes stand alongside postwar ranch houses, and that variety between individual homes is the defining feature of this market.

What buyers should know: Within Willow Glen, homes can differ widely in age, lot shape, parking, quality of work and walkability. With older homes, also look at the foundation, drainage, termites, permit history and insurance cost.

What sellers should know: A single price per square foot does not work well here. Historic character, upkeep, outdoor space and proximity to busy streets all need to show up in the pricing analysis.

95126 | Rose Garden: older streets, a conservation area and the Diridon edge

Rose Garden lies west of Downtown, with The Alameda to the northeast and I-880 to the west. It takes its name from the Municipal Rose Garden at Naglee Ave and Dana Ave, and Shasta-Hanchett forms its eastern half. Homes are mainly from the first half of the twentieth century, and the city’s Hanchett and Hester Park Conservation Area holds a strong mix of Prairie, Spanish Revival and Craftsman houses. Rose Garden Branch Library is on Naglee Ave, the Rosicrucian Egyptian Museum is on Park Ave, and Diridon Station is a short way east. Being close to Downtown and rail is the draw, while older-home upkeep and SJC flight paths are the usual trade-offs.

What buyers should know: On older homes, focus on the foundation and seismic work, old wiring and pipes, the roof, and permits for past additions. One-car or detached garages are common, so see street parking for yourself. If a home is inside the Conservation Area, ask city planning what applies before changing the exterior. Schools here are mainly in San José Unified, whose new boundaries apply to incoming students from 2027–28, so confirm the full address and enrollment year with the district.

What sellers should know: Older houses, condos along the main roads, and newer condos and townhomes near Diridon draw different buyers and price differently, so find comparable sales within each group. Among older homes, the gap usually comes down to structural updates and how much original detail survives, which makes complete records and permits for foundation, wiring and plumbing work valuable. Near Diridon, expect questions about BART Phase II construction and answer them from VTA’s published information.

95128 | West / Central San Jose: Valley Fair, the hospitals and the Bascom corridor

95128 sits between West and Central San Jose, where I-280 meets I-880, with Stevens Creek Blvd, Winchester Blvd and Bascom Ave as the main roads. Santana Row and Westfield Valley Fair are on the north side, and Santa Clara Valley Medical Center and O’Connor Hospital are both in this ZIP code, so shopping and medical care are closer at hand than in most residential areas. Housing is mixed: newer condos around Santana Row, condos and townhomes along the main roads, and mostly single-family homes on the residential streets. With that range, plus pockets of unincorporated county land, comparisons need to go down to the street.

What buyers should know: First confirm whether the address is inside city limits or on county land. Burbank, along Bascom Ave, is an unincorporated community surrounded by San Jose, and permits and services there go through the county. Schools here involve Campbell Union SD, Luther Burbank SD and Campbell Union HSD among others, so confirm the full address with the district. For condos, check the assigned parking, HOA dues, reserves and special assessments. Near Valley Fair and I-280, see the traffic on a weekend and at rush hour.

What sellers should know: Condos, townhomes and single-family homes should not be priced together, and condos near Santana Row belong in a different set from those elsewhere in the ZIP code. Gather permit records from the right agency, county or city, depending on the parcel. The city approved Urban Village plans for Santana Row/Valley Fair, Winchester and Stevens Creek in 2017, so when buyers ask about changes nearby, answer from the city’s published information.

95129 | West San Jose: what sits behind the prices

95129 is one of West San Jose’s higher-priced ZIP codes, next to Cupertino, Saratoga and the job centers around Apple, with I-280 along the north and Lawrence Expressway, Saratoga Ave and Bollinger Rd as the everyday roads. Most homes are 1950s and 1960s single-story ranches with three or four bedrooms and two-car garages, many of them remodeled or expanded with a second floor, and condos and townhomes line the main roads. Calabazas Branch Library, Calabazas Park, Rainbow Park and Saratoga Creek Park are all in the area, and shopping runs along Saratoga Ave, De Anza Blvd and Stevens Creek Blvd. Buyers here are usually weighing a combination of location, school assignment, land value and long-term plans rather than square footage alone.

What buyers should know: A 95129 address does not automatically mean Cupertino schools. Elementary, middle and high school districts can change from one street to the next. Two homes of similar size can price very differently because of school boundaries, the lot, room to expand and nearby traffic.

What sellers should know: The ZIP-wide median is not a pricing tool. Start with recent sales inside the same school boundary, with a similar lot, age and level of finish.

95131 | North San Jose: townhomes, condos and holding costs

95131 is a key residential part of North San Jose, north of US-101 and mostly between I-880 and I-680, with Berryessa to the east and Milpitas to the north. It has single-family homes, townhomes and condos from different eras, and the newer townhomes and condos usually come with an HOA. Errands and medical visits tend to stay in the area or head to Milpitas, three freeways are close by, and the Berryessa/North San José BART station is not far. It suits buyers comparing the commute to nearby job centers against the cost of keeping a property up.

What buyers should know: With a townhome or condo, price is only part of the comparison. Review HOA dues, reserves, special assessments, insurance responsibilities, rental limits and how well the community is kept up.

What sellers should know: Price by ownership type, size, parking, HOA and sales in the same community. Putting single-family homes and condos in one comparison set can easily mislead.

95132 | Berryessa: established single-family neighborhoods, schools and transit

95132 sits in the northeast corner of San Jose and takes in many of Berryessa’s established neighborhoods, with I-680 running through the middle, the Diablo Range foothills to the east and Milpitas to the north. It offers classic single-family homes and foothill-edge streets. Berryessa Community Center, Berryessa Branch Library, Noble Park and Penitencia Creek Park anchor daily life, and groceries are mostly in the shopping centers along Berryessa Rd, Hostetter Rd and Capitol Ave. Buyers can sort by commute, school boundaries, home size and budget.

What buyers should know: School assignments are not uniform across 95132. Check elementary, middle and high school districts for each address. Homes near the Berryessa/North San José BART station and homes near the foothills also differ in transit, surroundings and construction.

What sellers should know: A 95132 single-family home should not be priced against 95131 townhomes or condos. Use the same home type, lot size, school boundary, level of finish and recent sales.

95135 | Evergreen: newer foothill neighborhoods and what they cost to hold

95135 covers the eastern side of Evergreen in southeast San Jose, against the Diablo Range foothills, with San Felipe Rd and Yerba Buena Rd as the main roads. Most homes are two-story houses from the 1990s and 2000s with plenty of bedrooms and two- or three-car garages, while The Villages is a gated, age-restricted community for residents 55 and over. Evergreen Village Square, Village Square Branch Library, Evergreen Community Center and Evergreen Valley College are all in this ZIP code. There is no freeway or rail station in the area, so trips start on surface arterials out to US-101, and that is the main thing to weigh against the extra space.

What buyers should know: Work out holding costs home by home. Newer neighborhoods may carry HOA dues and CFD or Mello-Roos assessments, and only the tax bill and HOA documents will confirm amounts and end dates. For addresses near the hills, check CAL FIRE hazard zones and the state fault and landslide layers, and get an insurance quote before you offer. The Villages has its own age and community rules, so read the documents before buying. Schools in this area fall under Evergreen School District and East Side Union HSD; confirm the full address with each district.

What sellers should know: Sales here include regular single-family homes and age-restricted communities such as The Villages, so the ZIP-wide median is not a pricing tool, and comparable sales should stay within the same kind of community. Have HOA dues, the annual CFD amount and its end date ready, since buyers will build them into the monthly cost. On hillside lots, the view, driveway slope and insurance picture all belong in the pricing analysis.
