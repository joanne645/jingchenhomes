Why prices differ by ZIP code

San Jose is not a single real estate market. ZIP codes differ in home types, lots, school boundaries and buyer demand, and understanding those differences matters more than a price ranking.

Median price, sales and days on market for each ZIP code are being verified for a single period and will be published once confirmed.

ZIP code guides

95120 | Almaden Valley: space, hill views and holding costs

Almaden Valley sits at the south end of San Jose, a valley between the Santa Teresa Hills and the Sierra Azul, near the foothills and open space. Nearly all homes here are single-family, most built from the 1960s to the 1980s, with lots and garages generally roomier than in central neighborhoods. Almaden Lake Park, the Los Alamitos Creek Trail and Almaden Quicksilver County Park are close by, while everyday shopping usually means driving. Buyers tend to focus on home size, yards and the neighborhood setting, and on the commute trade-off to Silicon Valley’s main job centers.

What buyers should know: Check insurance availability, foothill fire risk, drainage, retaining walls, the roof and upkeep costs. Conditions vary a lot by location and property, so the whole ZIP cannot be treated as one risk level.

What sellers should know: Separate standard homes, large-lot properties, view homes and high-end remodels. With few high-end sales, a single month’s median can move simply because of which homes sold.

95124 | Cambrian: classic single-family homes and a workable commute

95124 covers much of the Cambrian area, with established low-rise neighborhoods. Buyers usually compare classic single-family homes, yard space, how much updating a home needs, and the commute.

What buyers should know: Look hard at the roof, drainage, plumbing, electrical and foundation, and confirm that any additions were permitted. A new kitchen and floors do not tell you those things. School boundaries inside Cambrian also need checking address by address.

What sellers should know: For classic homes here, a pre-listing inspection, needed repairs, sensible staging and a sharp set of comparable sales usually do more than a general “great schools” pitch.

95125 | Willow Glen: established streets and homes with character

Willow Glen sits southwest of Downtown, roughly between I-280, SR-87 and Los Gatos Creek, and locally the name usually means the older streets on either side of Lincoln Avenue. It is known for established residential streets, homes from many eras, and the shops and restaurants around Lincoln Avenue. Early twentieth-century bungalows, Tudors and Spanish Revival homes stand alongside postwar ranch houses, and that variety between individual homes is the defining feature of this market.

What buyers should know: Within Willow Glen, homes can differ widely in age, lot shape, parking, quality of work and walkability. With older homes, also look at the foundation, drainage, termites, permit history and insurance cost.

What sellers should know: A single price per square foot does not work well here. Historic character, upkeep, outdoor space and proximity to busy streets all need to show up in the pricing analysis.

95129 | West San Jose: what sits behind the prices

95129 is one of West San Jose’s higher-priced ZIP codes, next to Cupertino, Saratoga and the job centers around Apple. Buyers here are usually weighing a combination of location, school assignment, land value and long-term plans rather than square footage alone.

What buyers should know: A 95129 address does not automatically mean Cupertino schools. Elementary, middle and high school districts can change from one street to the next. Two homes of similar size can price very differently because of school boundaries, the lot, room to expand and nearby traffic.

What sellers should know: The ZIP-wide median is not a pricing tool. Start with recent sales inside the same school boundary, with a similar lot, age and level of finish.

95131 | North San Jose: townhomes, condos and holding costs

95131 is a key residential part of North San Jose, with single-family homes, townhomes and condos from different eras. It suits buyers comparing the commute to nearby job centers against the cost of keeping a property up.

What buyers should know: With a townhome or condo, price is only part of the comparison. Review HOA dues, reserves, special assessments, insurance responsibilities, rental limits and how well the community is kept up.

What sellers should know: Price by ownership type, size, parking, HOA and sales in the same community. Putting single-family homes and condos in one comparison set can easily mislead.

See 95131 details →

95132 | Berryessa: established single-family neighborhoods, schools and transit

95132 sits in the northeast corner of San Jose and takes in many of Berryessa’s established neighborhoods. It offers classic single-family homes and foothill-edge streets, so buyers can sort by commute, school boundaries, home size and budget.

What buyers should know: School assignments are not uniform across 95132. Check elementary, middle and high school districts for each address. Homes near the Berryessa/North San José BART station and homes near the foothills also differ in transit, surroundings and construction.

What sellers should know: A 95132 single-family home should not be priced against 95131 townhomes or condos. Use the same home type, lot size, school boundary, level of finish and recent sales.
