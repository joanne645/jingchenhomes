95132 | Berryessa: established single-family neighborhoods, schools and transit

95132 sits in the northeast corner of San Jose and takes in many of Berryessa’s established neighborhoods, with I-680 running through the middle, the Diablo Range foothills to the east and Milpitas to the north. It offers classic single-family homes and foothill-edge streets. Berryessa Community Center, Berryessa Branch Library, Noble Park and Penitencia Creek Park anchor daily life, and groceries are mostly in the shopping centers along Berryessa Rd, Hostetter Rd and Capitol Ave. Buyers can sort by commute, school boundaries, home size and budget.

East of I-680 toward the hills, most homes are single-family tracts from the 1960s through the 1980s, one or two stories on regular lots. West of I-680 and around the BART station, newer townhomes and condos are taller, often with tandem garages and HOA dues. They share a neighborhood name but live and cost very differently.

The Berryessa/North San José BART station is at 1620 Berryessa Rd, with VTA buses on site and first-come VTA-managed parking. VTA’s Orange Line light rail runs along Capitol Ave and connects to BART at Milpitas; note that the light-rail Berryessa stop is not the BART station. By car, I-680 heads north to Milpitas and Fremont, while US-101 or I-880 reach North San Jose and the Peninsula. Penitencia Creek Trail is the everyday walking and cycling route toward Alum Rock Park.

What buyers should know: School assignments are not uniform across 95132. Check elementary, middle and high school districts for each address. Homes near the Berryessa/North San José BART station and homes near the foothills also differ in transit, surroundings and construction.

Most of the area falls in Berryessa Union School District (TK–8), with high schools in East Side Union High School District, while some addresses toward US-101 are in Orchard School District. Berryessa Union closed Cherrywood, Toyon and Laneview elementary schools starting in 2025–26. If a listing still names one, rely on the district lookup.

On older homes, look at the roof, plumbing and windows. On newer townhomes near the station, check HOA dues, guest parking, stairs and any special assessments such as Mello-Roos on the tax bill. The BART station’s own address is in 95133, so walk the real route from the home to the platform. The BART tracks, I-680 and US-101 all bring noise. For lots near the east hills, check the state fault and landslide maps and CAL FIRE hazard maps, and ask about insurance early.

What sellers should know: A 95132 single-family home should not be priced against 95131 townhomes or condos. Use the same home type, lot size, school boundary, level of finish and recent sales.

The San Jose Flea Market site on Berryessa Rd was approved in June 2021 for transit-oriented development, so nearby residents should expect construction at some point. When a listing touches on changes like this, rely on the city’s published information.

Berryessa in depth

Location and boundaries

Berryessa is in northeast San Jose, running from around US-101 and Coyote Creek on the west to the Diablo Range foothills on the east, with Milpitas to the north and I-680 down the middle. Berryessa Rd, Hostetter Rd, Capitol Ave and Piedmont Rd are the main roads. It is one of the city’s 15 Planning Areas and in everyday use roughly matches ZIP codes 95132 and 95133, among others. The name is not a legal boundary, and more than one school district operates inside it.

Homes

East of I-680 toward the foothills, you’ll mostly find single-family tracts from the 1960s through the 1980s, one or two stories on regular lots. West of I-680 and around the BART station, newer townhomes and condos are taller, often with tandem garages and HOA dues. On the older houses, check whether the roof, plumbing and windows have been updated. On the newer townhomes, go item by item through HOA dues, guest parking rules, the number of stairs, and whether the tax bill carries a special assessment such as Mello-Roos.

Daily life

Berryessa Community Center is at 3050 Berryessa Rd, and Berryessa Branch Library is at 3355 Noble Ave, right next to Noble Park. Penitencia Creek Park (3027 Penitencia Creek Rd) and the creekside Penitencia Creek Trail are the main route for walks and bike rides. City data shows the trail segments running east from N King Rd past I-680 and Capitol Ave to Piedmont Rd, leading toward Alum Rock Park (15350 Penitencia Creek Rd), a city regional park with canyon trails and picnic areas; check the city’s page for vehicle entry hours and fees. Cataldi Park (1765 Bethany Ave) and Flickinger Park are also here. Groceries are mostly in the shopping centers along Berryessa Rd, Hostetter Rd and Capitol Ave, including Chinese and other Asian supermarkets. Stores turn over often, so see what’s open when you’re in the area.

Commute and schools

The Berryessa/North San José BART station is at 1620 Berryessa Rd, with VTA-managed parking sold by the day or month on a first-come basis, and VTA buses on site, including Rapid 500 to Downtown and Diridon. VTA’s Orange Line light rail also runs along Capitol Ave, with stops including Hostetter, Berryessa and Penitencia Creek, and connects to BART at Milpitas; note that the light-rail Berryessa stop and the Berryessa/North San José BART station are two different places. By car, I-680 goes north to Milpitas and Fremont, and US-101 or I-880 reach North San Jose and the Peninsula. Most of the area is in Berryessa Union School District (TK–8), with high schools in East Side Union High School District, including Piedmont Hills and Independence here. On December 18, 2024, the Berryessa Union board voted to close Cherrywood, Toyon and Laneview elementary schools starting in 2025–26, and the district’s website now lists seven elementary and three middle schools. Some addresses toward US-101 are in Orchard School District. Both districts look up schools by address, and if a listing still names a closed school, go by the district’s tool.

The trade-offs

Berryessa’s strength is having BART, light rail and two freeways close by. The trade-off is that new homes near the stations are mostly multi-story townhomes with HOA dues, while single-family homes with yards sit farther from the train, so plan how you’ll cover that last stretch. The BART tracks, I-680 and US-101 all bring noise. The San Jose Flea Market site on Berryessa Rd was approved in June 2021 for transit-oriented development, with about five acres kept for the market. According to the owner’s October 2025 quarterly report cited on a city page, no closure notice would go out before March 31, 2026, and the schedule after that is still undetermined, so anyone living nearby should expect construction at some point. For lots near the east hills, check the state’s fault and landslide layers and CAL FIRE hazard maps.

What to look for on a visit

On a weekday morning, go to the BART parking lot to see how spaces actually fill up, then travel from the home to the platform the way you plan to do it every day. In the evening, watch the traffic where Berryessa Rd meets I-680 and along Capitol Ave. At a townhome near the station, listen for trains and count the guest parking spaces. At a single-family home to the east, drive a stretch of Piedmont Rd to get a sense of the grades and the route to Alum Rock Park.
