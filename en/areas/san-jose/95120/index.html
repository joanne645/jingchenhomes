95120 | Almaden Valley: space, hill views and holding costs

Almaden Valley sits at the south end of San Jose, a valley between the Santa Teresa Hills and the Sierra Azul, near the foothills and open space. Nearly all homes here are single-family, most built from the 1960s to the 1980s, with lots and garages generally roomier than in central neighborhoods. Almaden Lake Park, the Los Alamitos Creek Trail and Almaden Quicksilver County Park are close by, while everyday shopping usually means driving. Buyers tend to focus on home size, yards and the neighborhood setting, and on the commute trade-off to Silicon Valley’s main job centers.

What buyers should know: Check insurance availability, foothill fire risk, drainage, retaining walls, the roof and upkeep costs. Conditions vary a lot by location and property, so the whole ZIP cannot be treated as one risk level.

What sellers should know: Separate standard homes, large-lot properties, view homes and high-end remodels. With few high-end sales, a single month’s median can move simply because of which homes sold.

Almaden Valley in depth

Location and boundaries

Almaden Valley is at the far southern end of San Jose, a valley between the Santa Teresa Hills and the Sierra Azul range. Almaden Expressway and Camden Ave are the two main north-south roads, and the north end meets Blossom Valley and Cambrian near SR-85. Almaden is one of the city’s 15 Planning Areas and roughly matches ZIP code 95120 in everyday use; farther south, New Almaden is the old mining settlement. These are planning and customary names rather than legal boundaries, and they do not equal school attendance areas.

Homes

Nearly everything here is a single-family home, most built from the 1960s to the 1980s, typically single-story ranches or two-story floor plans with four or five bedrooms, on lots and with garages generally roomier than in central neighborhoods. The hillside edge has sloped and irregular lots, plus some gated HOA communities. For homes of this age, check whether the roof, HVAC, original aluminum windows and plumbing have been updated. Hillside homes also call for a look at drainage, retaining walls and geotechnical reports.

Daily life

Public facilities cluster on Camden Ave, where Almaden Community Center and Almaden Branch Library share the site at 6445 Camden Ave; the library’s page lists more than 100 parking spaces and VTA routes 64B and 83. Almaden Lake Park, a city regional park near Almaden Expy and Coleman Rd, is where the Guadalupe River Trail and Los Alamitos Creek Trail connect, and the latter follows the creek south past Via Valiente and Camden Ave. Guadalupe Oak Grove Park (entrances near Thorntree Dr and Starwood Dr) is mostly oak woodland, and neighboring Jeffery Fontana Park has a dog park. At the valley’s south end, Almaden Quicksilver County Park has its main Hacienda entrance at 21785 Almaden Rd, a second entrance at Mockingbird Hill, more than 30 miles of trails and no entry fee. Groceries and everyday shopping are at the centers along Almaden Expy and Blossom Hill Rd, nearly always by car.

Commute and schools

Most trips out of the valley take Almaden Expressway north to SR-85, then on to SR-87 or SR-17. The farther south you live, the longer the surface-road stretch before the freeway, and both rush hours funnel onto the same road. VTA’s current station list has no Almaden station; the closest light rail stop is Ohlone-Chynoweth on the Blue Line, with more than 500 parking spaces, and bus 64A runs from there along Almaden Expy while 64B reaches Almaden and Camden. Schools come in two parts. Most of the valley is in San José Unified (TK–12), whose list here includes Leland High (6677 Camden Ave), Bret Harte Middle, Castillero Middle, and Graystone, Williams, Simonds and Los Alamitos elementary schools. Some streets at the north end near SR-85 are in Union School District for elementary and middle grades (its Guadalupe Elementary at 6044 Vera Cruz Dr is also in 95120), with high school in Campbell Union High School District. The schools here are not on the closure list in San José Unified’s March 2026 merger plan, but attendance boundaries change for new students starting in 2027–28, so check by address and enrollment year.

The trade-offs

Almaden offers space, quiet and hiking trails close to home. The cost is its southern location: nearly every trip toward North San Jose or the Peninsula goes through Almaden Expy and SR-85 first, with few alternatives and limited transit. Lots near the hills have the views, but they also take an extra round of homework, including CAL FIRE hazard maps, the state’s fault and landslide layers, and the insurance quotes that follow from them.

What to look for on a visit

Pick a weekday morning rush hour, drive from the home to the SR-85 on-ramp and note how the surface stretch actually goes, then make the reverse trip that evening. On a weekend, stop by Almaden Lake Park and the Hacienda entrance to Almaden Quicksilver to see the parking and crowds. At a foothill home, walk the back of the lot to look at the slope, drainage channels and retaining walls, and note how close the surrounding vegetation comes to the house.
