95135 | Evergreen: newer foothill neighborhoods and what they cost to hold

95135 covers the eastern side of Evergreen in southeast San Jose, against the Diablo Range foothills, with San Felipe Rd and Yerba Buena Rd as the main roads. Most homes are two-story houses from the 1990s and 2000s with plenty of bedrooms and two- or three-car garages, while The Villages is a gated, age-restricted community for residents 55 and over. Evergreen Village Square, Village Square Branch Library, Evergreen Community Center and Evergreen Valley College are all in this ZIP code. There is no freeway or rail station in the area, so trips start on surface arterials out to US-101, and that is the main thing to weigh against the extra space.

What buyers should know: Work out holding costs home by home. Newer neighborhoods may carry HOA dues and CFD or Mello-Roos assessments, and only the tax bill and HOA documents will confirm amounts and end dates. For addresses near the hills, check CAL FIRE hazard zones and the state fault and landslide layers, and get an insurance quote before you offer. The Villages has its own age and community rules, so read the documents before buying. Schools in this area fall under Evergreen School District and East Side Union HSD; confirm the full address with each district.

What sellers should know: Sales here include regular single-family homes and age-restricted communities such as The Villages, so the ZIP-wide median is not a pricing tool, and comparable sales should stay within the same kind of community. Have HOA dues, the annual CFD amount and its end date ready, since buyers will build them into the monthly cost. On hillside lots, the view, driveway slope and insurance picture all belong in the pricing analysis.

Evergreen in depth

Location and boundaries

Evergreen is in southeast San Jose, backed by the Diablo Range foothills, with US-101 roughly as its western edge and Tully Rd and Lake Cunningham to the north. Capitol Expressway, Aborn Rd, Yerba Buena Rd, San Felipe Rd and Silver Creek Rd are the main roads. It is one of the city’s 15 Planning Areas and in everyday use covers ZIP codes including 95121 and 95135. People often split it into the older west side near US-101, the area around Evergreen Village Square, and the Silver Creek hillside communities. These are planning or customary terms rather than legal boundaries.

Homes

Home ages vary widely. The west side near US-101 and Capitol Expy is mostly 1970s single-family homes, and homes get newer as you move east and uphill, where two-story houses from the 1990s and 2000s predominate, with plenty of bedrooms and two- or three-car garages. In the hills of neighboring 95138 is the gated Silver Creek Valley Country Club community around a golf course, and in the southeast corner The Villages is an age-restricted 55-and-over community. Both have HOAs and their own rules, so get the HOA documents and a full fee breakdown before buying. City park data classifies many pocket parks around Evergreen Village Square as CFD (Community Facilities District) parks, so tax bills in these newer neighborhoods may carry a related special assessment; check each home’s bill.

Daily life

Evergreen Village Square is the community hub on the east side, with Village Square Branch Library at 4001 Evergreen Village Square. On the west side, Evergreen Branch Library is at 2635 Aborn Rd. Evergreen Community Center shares 4860 San Felipe Rd with Evergreen Park. Lake Cunningham Park (2305 S White Rd) is a city regional park with an Action Sports Park; check the city’s page for parking fees and hours. Montgomery Hill Park adjoins Evergreen Valley College, and the Evergreen Creek Trail runs there from Falls Creek Dr. City trail data lists the Thompson Creek Trail as existing from Tully Rd past Quimby Rd to Aborn Rd, with several segments farther south still planned. Groesbeck Hill Park is at 2646 Klein Rd. Major shopping is around Eastridge at the north edge, and everyday groceries are in the centers along Aborn Rd, Capitol Expy and San Felipe Rd.

Commute and schools

No freeway runs through Evergreen, so nearly every trip starts on Capitol Expressway, Yerba Buena Rd, Aborn Rd or Tully Rd out to US-101, then on to I-280, I-680 or SR-85. The closer you live to the hills, the longer that surface stretch. On the rail side, VTA is building the Eastridge to BART Regional Connector, extending light rail about 2.4 miles from Alum Rock station on an elevated guideway in the Capitol Expy median to Eastridge Transit Center, with a Story Road station in between. Work began in spring 2024, and VTA’s September 2026 project update says the elevated structure is more than 90 percent done, with track and testing next and full completion expected in early 2028; go by VTA’s announcements for the opening date. Elementary and middle grades are in Evergreen School District (TK–8), whose website lists 13 elementary schools and three middle schools, Chaboya, LeyVa and Quimby Oak. The district closed Laurelwood and Dove Hill elementary schools at the end of 2019–20, and those names may still show up in old listing data. High schools are in East Side Union High School District, including Evergreen Valley High and Silver Creek High here. Each district has its own address lookup.

The trade-offs

What you usually get in Evergreen is a newer, larger single-family home. The trade-off is mainly transportation: with no nearby freeway on-ramp, trips to North San Jose, the Peninsula or the East Bay all begin on the same few surface arterials, and rail options stay limited until the light rail extension opens. Hillside neighborhoods have wide-open views, but check CAL FIRE hazard maps and the state’s fault and landslide layers, and count insurance quotes, HOA dues and any CFD assessment in your holding costs.

What to look for on a visit

During the weekday morning rush, drive from the home to the US-101 on-ramp at Capitol Expy or Yerba Buena Rd and note which intersections back up. In the evening, walk around Evergreen Village Square and see how parking and the surrounding streets work. Pass the light rail construction on Capitol Expy to see how close it is to the home. At a hillside house, look at the driveway grade, the slope behind the home and the nearby vegetation, and then look up the fire and geology layers by address.
