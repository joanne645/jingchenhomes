95131 | North San Jose: townhomes, condos and holding costs

95131 is a key residential part of North San Jose, north of US-101 and mostly between I-880 and I-680, with Berryessa to the east and Milpitas to the north. It has single-family homes, townhomes and condos from different eras, and the newer townhomes and condos usually come with an HOA. Errands and medical visits tend to stay in the area or head to Milpitas, three freeways are close by, and the Berryessa/North San José BART station is not far. It suits buyers comparing the commute to nearby job centers against the cost of keeping a property up.

What buyers should know: With a townhome or condo, price is only part of the comparison. Review HOA dues, reserves, special assessments, insurance responsibilities, rental limits and how well the community is kept up.

What sellers should know: Price by ownership type, size, parking, HOA and sales in the same community. Putting single-family homes and condos in one comparison set can easily mislead.

North San Jose in depth

Location and boundaries

95131 is north of US-101 and mostly between I-880 and I-680, in what people call North San Jose, with Berryessa to the east and Milpitas to the north. North San Jose and Berryessa are customary names with no legal boundary, and a ZIP code does not define a school attendance area. The city’s Envision San José 2040 general plan names North San Jose as a focus for new housing, so this area is still changing.

Homes

You’ll find single-family homes, townhomes and condos from different eras, and newer three-story townhomes and condos are a signature of North San Jose. Townhomes typically trade stairs, tandem garages and small yards for newer construction and systems. When buying one, review HOA dues, reserves, special assessments, insurance responsibilities, rental limits and guest parking. For single-family homes, check the roof, plumbing and windows by age, and verify permits for additions.

Daily life

Everyday errands and medical visits tend to stay in the area or go to neighboring cities. North San Jose residents often head to Milpitas, and there are shopping centers along Berryessa Rd and Capitol Ave; check what’s open now. Public facilities are spread out, so look up the nearest branch in the library’s table by address; Berryessa Branch Library is at 3355 Noble Ave. To the east, Regional Medical Center (225 N Jackson Ave) has a 24-hour emergency department and Level II trauma center, and your insurer can confirm whether it’s in network.

Commute and schools

US-101, I-880 and I-680 are all close, and Montague Expressway is a commonly used arterial here. For BART, use Berryessa/North San José station (1620 Berryessa Rd, ZIP 95133), with paid, first-come parking run by VTA. VTA’s Blue Line runs along North First St, and with most of 95131 east of I-880, how you’d reach it depends on the address. Berryessa Union School District’s Brooktree (1781 Olivetree Dr) and Vinci Park (1311 Vinci Park Way) elementary schools and single-school Orchard School District (921 Fox Ln, K–8) are all in 95131, with high schools in East Side Union HSD, and some North San Jose addresses are in Santa Clara Unified. Confirm every school by full address with the district.

The trade-offs

You’re close to the North San Jose and Milpitas job centers and three freeways, with plenty of home types to choose from. You accept the stairs and HOA costs of townhome living, and some blocks sit right next to major roads. US-101, I-880 and I-680 bring noise, and the airport’s WebTrak tool shows actual flight tracks for an address. New housing is being steered here, so more mid-rise projects may follow. Liquefaction zones are common on the valley floor and some addresses need a FEMA flood map check, so run these layers by address.

What to look for on a visit

During the weekday morning rush, compare the routes from the home to the US-101, I-880 and I-680 on-ramps, then check the trip to your actual office entrance. Go to the BART parking lot to see how spaces fill, or walk the real route to the platform. At a townhome, count the stairs and guest spaces, and try your own car in the garage. Afterward, look up the address in FEMA’s flood maps, CGS EQ Zapp and the airport’s WebTrak.
