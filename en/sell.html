IN DETAIL

What to think through when selling

Start with three kinds of information

  • The home: address, type, known condition, recent maintenance or changes, and existing documents.
  • The timing: when you hope to list and move out, and whether you are also buying your next home.
  • Your priorities: how you rank price, preparation spending, showing flexibility and certainty of closing.

Pricing: put the home in the right comparison set

Comparable sales need to reflect location, home type, size, lot, condition and timing. Citywide numbers cannot be turned directly into the price of one home, and a list price is not a sale price. A pricing conversation should look at recent sales, current competition and your listing date together.

Online estimates are a useful first reference, but a specific home needs its details checked. Jing can talk through your home's condition and the right comparison set so you understand the basis for a price.

Preparation: rank by impact and budget

From cleaning and decluttering to necessary maintenance and possible repairs, list the issues before deciding what to spend. Each item should answer three questions: what does it improve, what will it cost in money and time, and does it affect the listing schedule? No renovation should be assumed to return its cost or more.

Presentation and marketing: help buyers understand the home accurately

Based on the home's features, discuss photos, written descriptions, flow and showing arrangements. What you highlight should come from real facts, such as how rooms are used, outdoor space and verified maintenance. Specific presentation plans, services and fees are agreed when you work together.

Comparing offers: weigh price and execution together

Offers can differ in financing, timeline, contingencies and other terms. Put them in one table and weigh them against your moving plan. Jing can help organize the discussion points. Specific rights, duties and deadlines are governed by the transaction documents and appropriate professional advice.

Getting ready to close: leave real room for the move

Gather home documents, items to hand over, moving plans and anything else you will need to do. If you are also buying, coordinate funds and temporary housing rather than building everything around one ideal closing date.

Common questions before selling

Do I have to renovate before selling?

Not necessarily. It depends on the home's condition, what likely buyers need, your budget and your timing. Start with maintenance, decluttering and presentation, then assess whether bigger changes are worth it.

Can I use a website estimate as my list price?

Automated estimates may not fully reflect condition, exact location or recent changes. Treat one as a single reference and analyze it alongside suitable comparable homes and what is actually on site.

When moving, should I buy first or sell first?

It depends on your financing, the bridge costs you can accept, how the sale of your current home is arranged and the home you want next. List the cash and timing needs for each order, then discuss them with your agent and a lending professional.

What costs should I plan for?

Consider preparation and repairs, presentation, agreed brokerage services, title and closing, loan payoff, moving and any applicable taxes. Whether each applies, who pays and how much depend on the home, the agreement and the transaction. Check tax matters with the right professional.
