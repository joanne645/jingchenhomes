Living in Dublin, California: Compare the BART Link and the Tax Bill

Dublin offers newer, roomier homes in much of the city. The trade-offs are hotter inland summers, commutes that lean on freeways or BART connections, and HOA dues and added taxes that need checking home by home.

Two BART stations, the I-580 and I-680 interchange, newer eastern communities, and large sports parks

This is a concise English edition. The full guide in Chinese includes maps, more detail and a half-day tour route.

City overview

Dublin is in the Tri-Valley, on the north side of I-580, with the I-680 interchange at its southwest corner. Pleasanton lies across I-580, San Ramon in Contra Costa County is to the north along I-680, and Livermore is to the east past a stretch of unincorporated county land. Dublin Blvd runs just north of I-580 across the whole city, from the western foothills to Fallon Rd. West of the city's middle are older neighborhoods and Downtown; to the east are planned communities and large parks built in phases.

Dublin has no general hospital with an emergency room, so the nearest ones are in Pleasanton and San Ramon. Both BART stations sit in the I-580 median on the Dublin-Pleasanton line, and the Dublin/Pleasanton station's official address is in Pleasanton, so near BART in a listing should be translated into which side you enter from and by what route. The area names below come from local usage, project names and Specific Plans; they are not legal boundaries or school attendance areas.

Neighborhoods

These names are common usage, not legal boundaries.

West Dublin Foothills and Schaefer Ranch

West of I-680 and San Ramon Rd, the flatter eastern part has older one- and two-story homes, usually with two-car garages and backyards, while Schaefer Ranch at the end of Dublin Blvd is a much newer hillside community. Shannon Park, Schaefer Ranch Park and Dublin Hills Regional Park are close, and both freeways are nearby, but shops and stations get farther away as you go west, and Schaefer Ranch has essentially one way in, along Dublin Blvd. Budget for older-home updates, and on slopes check drainage, retaining walls, slope maintenance duties and any special tax assessments.

Downtown Dublin and Central Older Neighborhoods

East of I-680, west of Dougherty Rd and north of I-580, this area combines the Downtown Specific Plan's shopping district with older streets of mainly single-story homes on regular lots, many expanded, plus newer apartments and condos near West Dublin/Pleasanton BART that are often rentals. Dublin Place, the library, City Hall, Dublin Sports Grounds and Heritage Park are short trips, and BART and both freeways are close. Expect traffic in the commercial area and steady noise near I-680, I-580 and BART, while homes tend to have little or no HOA and simpler tax bills, which you should still confirm.

Dublin Crossing (Boulevard) and the Camp Parks Edge

East of Dougherty Rd and north of Dublin Blvd on former Camp Parks land, this newer development is nearly all townhomes, condos and some small-lot single-family homes, with three-story plans and ground-floor garages common. The 30-acre Don Biddle Community Park, opened in September 2022, is the neighborhood's main shared space, and the Iron Horse Trail connects toward Dublin/Pleasanton BART. Plan for stairs, small yards, HOA dues and possible special taxes, plus occasional noise from the active training area to the north.

East Dublin: Emerald Glen and Dublin Ranch

From Arnold Rd and Hacienda Dr east to Fallon Rd, this area has condos near BART and Dublin Blvd, townhomes and small-lot houses to the north, and larger homes on the slopes, including Positano. Emerald Glen Park with The Wave, plus Hacienda Crossings, Persimmon Place and other shopping centers, make it the first stop for many Dublin buyers. Expect peak congestion on Tassajara Rd and the I-580 ramps, widespread HOAs, and tax-bill items that differ between builders and phases.

East of Fallon Rd and North Tassajara Rd Communities

The city's newest area includes Jordan Ranch, Wallis Ranch and Tassajara Hills, mainly two- and three-story homes and townhomes from the past decade on compact lots, many with gates or clubhouses. Fallon Sports Park and Wallis Ranch Community Park anchor weekend activity, and daily shopping is at Fallon Gateway and Grafton Station. This is the farthest area from BART, some land nearby is still under construction, HOA and tax-bill items tend to be higher than in older areas, and addresses near the eastern edge should review wildfire maps.

Home types

Dublin's housing divides roughly at Dougherty Rd. To the west is the older city, mainly one- and two-story single-family homes. To the east are phased planned communities mixing single-family homes, townhomes and condos, getting newer as you go east. Older homes call for attention to foundation, drainage, roof age, plumbing, electrical panel, windows, insulation and addition permits. Newer homes call for attention to stairs, bedroom layout, side-by-side or tandem garages, guest parking, yard slope and what the HOA does and does not cover. In hillside communities, ask who owns retaining walls and slopes, how rainwater drains, and how much flat yard you really get.

Single-family homes

Older one- and two-story homes in the west and center usually have larger lots and little or no HOA. Newer two-story homes in Dublin Ranch, Positano, Jordan Ranch and Tassajara Hills have compact lots and usually an HOA.

Townhomes

Concentrated in Dublin Crossing, eastern communities north of Dublin Blvd and Wallis Ranch. Three-story plans with ground-floor garages are common, so walk the path from garage to kitchen and from primary bedroom to laundry.

Condos

Mainly around the two BART stations and along Dublin Blvd, along with many rental-only apartment buildings. Confirm the ownership type first, then compare what HOA dues cover and how parking is assigned.

Transit and commuting

I-580 runs along the southern edge, west through Castro Valley toward Oakland and east through Livermore over Altamont Pass, and it carries heavy westbound traffic in the morning and eastbound traffic in the evening. I-680 runs north to San Ramon and Walnut Creek and south through Pleasanton and the Sunol Grade toward Fremont and San Jose.

BART's line starts at Dublin/Pleasanton and stops at West Dublin/Pleasanton on its way to Oakland and San Francisco. The last leg often matters most: the stations sit in the median, so walking or biking in means bridges and big intersections.

Schools and official lookups

Dublin Unified School District serves the whole city from preschool and TK through grade 12, so you do not need to sort out separate elementary and high school districts. Living in Dublin only tells you the district, though. The specific elementary, middle and high schools depend on which attendance boundary an address falls in, so check the full street address through the district's official lookup, and do not rely on which campus looks closest.

Boundary results do not guarantee placement. DUSD describes a diversion process: when a grade has no space at the assigned school, a student may be placed at another school with room. Near the boundary between Dublin High and Emerald High, ask the district directly which school applies to that address now and whether changes are planned.

Daily life

Shopping lines Dublin Blvd in three groups: Dublin Place and nearby centers east of I-680; Hacienda Crossings, Persimmon Place and The Shops at Waterford in the middle; and Grafton Station and Fallon Gateway at Fallon Rd.

Parks are a strong part of the newer planning. The city lists more than 20, with Emerald Glen, Fallon Sports Park, Don Biddle and Dublin Sports Grounds as the largest, and parking can get tight on busy game days. For medical care, the nearest general hospitals are Stanford Health Care Tri-Valley in Pleasanton and San Ramon Regional Medical Center. Check your insurance network first, and map the route from both ends of the city.

Environment checks

Check hazard layers by address before you get attached to a home. For earthquakes, use the California Geological Survey's EQ Zapp to see whether the address is in a fault, liquefaction or earthquake-induced landslide zone, especially in the western foothills and along I-680. For wildfire, use CAL FIRE's Fire Hazard Severity Zones viewer, since new local responsibility area maps were released in 2025; western slopes and the eastern and northern city edges deserve a look. For flooding, confirm the zone on FEMA's map, particularly near creeks. These maps are drawn by standard methods for disclosure, insurance and building rules, so they do not equal the actual condition of a home.
