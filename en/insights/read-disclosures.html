Reading Disclosures: TDS, SPQ, NHD and More

Disclosures are the basis for your price and terms. Read them early and turn anything unclear into written questions.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not legal advice. Your rights and duties depend on your contract and applicable law; please consult an attorney.

What disclosures are

Disclosures are the documents sellers and agents must, or customarily do, provide about a home's condition, hazard zones and known issues. In the Bay Area you can often read them before writing an offer, and they are the basis for deciding your price and terms.

The main documents

DocumentWhat it isWhat to look for
TDS (Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement)Statutory form under Civil Code §1102 et seq., generally for one-to-four-unit homes, with some exemptions"Yes" answers, explanations, the agent sections
SPQ (Seller Property Questionnaire)C.A.R. form that goes further than the TDSRepairs, leaks, disputes, neighbors, noise, remodels
NHD (Natural Hazard Disclosure)Flood, dam inundation, fire hazard, fault and seismic hazard zonesEach in-or-out answer, then insurance effects
Preliminary Title ReportThe title company's initial reportEasements, liens, who holds title
HOA documentsGoverning documents, budget, reserve study, fees, open issuesReserve strength, special assessments, litigation
Inspection reportsSeller's advance home and pest reportsWhat was found and what was fixed
Other required noticesLead paint for pre-1978 homes, Mello-Roos, supplemental tax, Megan's Law, smoke detector and water heater statements, earthquake guideWhich apply to this home

Recent requirements

  • Electrical: since January 1, 2026, sellers must provide an electrical system statement (Civil Code §1102.6i).
  • Gas appliances: since January 1, 2026, sellers of single-family homes must disclose known state or local rules on future replacement of gas appliances (§1102.6j). C.A.R.'s December 2025 SPQ added questions on this and on tobacco or nicotine residue.
  • Fire hardening: sellers of pre-2010 homes in High or Very High fire hazard zones have owed fire-related disclosures since 2021, and since July 1, 2025 must also provide a checklist of low-cost hardening measures and say which are present. This requires disclosure, not upgrades.

Your rights if disclosures arrive late

Under Civil Code §1102.3, the TDS is due as soon as practicable before title transfers. If it, or a material amendment, arrives after you sign an offer, you can terminate by written notice within three days of in-person delivery or five days of mailing (five days as well for electronic delivery, where the parties agreed to transact electronically). That window comes from statute and is not a general cooling-off period. Other review periods come from your contract. The C.A.R. RPA form defaults to seller delivery within 7 days of acceptance, and buyer review until 17 days after acceptance or 5 days after receipt, whichever is later. Those periods can be changed.

How to read the package

  1. Check completeness. Are documents missing or answers blank?
  2. Mark every "yes" and every explanation. That's where the issues sit.
  3. Cross-check. If the TDS says no leaks and a report notes water staining, ask about it.
  4. Use official records. Confirm permits with the building department and zones with EQ Zapp and the OSFM zone page.
  5. List your questions and ask for written answers.

Common mistakes

  • Reading "seller is unaware" as "no problem." A disclosure reflects only what the seller knows.
  • Reading the NHD without calling an insurer.
  • Skipping the financial pages of the HOA package.
  • Assuming every sale comes with the same documents. Some transfers, such as court-ordered ones, can be exempt from the TDS.
This is general information, not legal advice. Requirements change, and these notes reflect what we verified as of November 2026. Ask an attorney or the right professional about your situation.

Next step

Ask for the disclosure package as soon as you are interested, and read it before discussing price. Jing can help turn anything unclear into a question list for the seller or a professional. For technical findings, see home inspection.
