How to Search for a Bay Area Home

Searching is about narrowing. Set your criteria first, then compare every home on the same sheet.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

Searching is about narrowing

Listing sites show what is for sale. Deciding whether a home deserves a visit takes criteria you set in advance. Clear criteria can get you to a decision in ten showings. Vague ones can leave you undecided after thirty.

Split your criteria into two columns

  • Must-haves: price ceiling, longest acceptable commute, bedrooms, parking, single-level or accessibility needs.
  • Nice-to-haves: garage size, orientation, yard, kitchen updates.

DRE's buyer checklist starts in the same place. Choose a property type early, too. Detached homes, townhomes and condos differ in HOA costs, maintenance duties and loan review.

How to run the search

  1. Search by map, not city name. Plot your workplace and regular stops, then narrow along those routes.
  2. Set alerts. See new listings quickly and notice how fast similar homes sell.
  3. Read the history. Days on market, price changes and prior sales are useful clues, but portal data can lag. Confirm it against MLS and public records.
  4. Review weekly. Note why each home failed and adjust your criteria.

For background, start with the areas page and city guides such as Fremont and San Ramon.

What to record at showings

LayoutStairs, garage-to-kitchen path, sound between bedrooms
SurroundingsFreeway, rail and flight-path noise; visit morning and evening
UpkeepSigns around the roof, plumbing, electrical panel, drainage and foundation
CostsHOA dues, added items on the tax bill, an insurance quote
RulesHOA limits on rentals, remodeling and parking

Showings and your agreement

California rules require a written buyer representation agreement as soon as practicable and no later than the offer, and NAR rules for MLS participants call for one before touring a home, including live virtual tours. If you walk into an open house on your own, the agent there acts for the seller. State regulations say so directly, which means anything you say about your budget or limit may reach the seller.

The agreement's term, exclusivity and compensation are all negotiable, and California caps each term at three months. Commissions are negotiable and not set by law.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting the school name on a listing. Marketing copy is no basis for enrollment. Use the district's official tools with the full address.
  • Trusting portal estimates. Zillow itself says the Zestimate is not an appraisal.
  • Deciding from photos. They don't show slope, smell or noise.
  • Asking who lives there. Under fair housing law, agents cannot describe or steer neighborhoods by race, religion or family status. Look at public data, walk the area yourself and decide on objective factors.
  • Waiting until offer day to read disclosures. Ask for them as soon as you are interested. What they say may change your price.
Listing details, square footage and school information can be wrong. Verify anything important with the right agency or professional.

Next step

Write your two columns, set alerts, tour three to five homes and use the table above. Then go back to comparing cities and decide whether to keep narrowing or change direction.
