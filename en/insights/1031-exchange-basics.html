1031 Exchange Basics: 45/180 Days, QIs and California's FTB 3840

A 1031 exchange can defer tax on an investment sale, but the deadlines are strict and it has to be set up before you close. These are the basic rules, plus California's extra filing.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not tax or legal advice. Please consult a CPA, tax professional or attorney about your situation.

What a 1031 exchange is

A 1031 exchange lets you swap real property held for investment or business use for other like-kind real property and postpone tax on the gain. The IRS is plain about it: the gain is tax-deferred, not tax-free. It carries into the new property and comes due when you eventually sell outside another exchange.

It matters because a missed deadline or touching the sale proceeds can make the entire gain taxable that year, with little room to fix it afterward.

What qualifies

  • Real property only. Since 2018 the federal rule covers only real property held for business or investment. California matches this for tax years beginning in 2025 or later.
  • "Like-kind" is broad. The focus is on why you hold the property. Real estate held primarily for resale does not qualify.
  • Not your home. Property used solely as a personal residence is out. So are vacation and second homes held mainly for personal use, and the IRS has warned about promoters pitching those.

Two hard deadlines

DeadlineRequirement
45 daysIdentify replacement property in writing after you transfer the old property, by street address or legal description
180 daysReceive the replacement by the earlier of 180 days after transfer or your tax return due date, including extensions

The IRS says these generally cannot be extended, apart from federally declared disasters. As an example, close on December 1, 2026 and day 45 is January 15, 2027, while day 180 is May 30. Without a filing extension, your deadline moves up to the April return date, which is easy to miss on a year-end sale.

You are also limited in how many properties you can identify: generally three of any value, or any number whose combined value is no more than double the property you gave up.

The qualified intermediary

In a deferred exchange, sale proceeds cannot pass through your hands. A qualified intermediary (QI) holds them. You cannot act as your own, and neither generally can your agent or anyone who served you in an advisory role in the past two years, which typically includes your attorney, accountant and broker. Publication 544 lists the exceptions. Jing Chen is a real estate agent, not a QI.

The IRS notes that some intermediaries have gone bankrupt, leaving clients past their deadlines. Ask how funds are held and whether they are segregated, and what bonding or insurance applies.

Boot and reporting

Cash or other non-like-kind property you receive, called boot, triggers gain up to that amount. Paying down debt can have the same effect, so have a CPA run the numbers in advance. Each exchange goes on Form 8824. With related parties, a disposition by either side within two years generally brings the deferred gain back.

California's extra layer

When you exchange California property for property in another state, California keeps tracking the deferred gain, often called the clawback. According to the Franchise Tax Board (FTB), as of November 2026:

  • You must file FTB 3840 for the year of the exchange and each later year until the California-source gain is recognized on a California return, regardless of where you live.
  • If you file neither FTB 3840 nor a return, the FTB may estimate income and issue a Notice of Proposed Assessment with penalties and interest.
  • Exchanges are generally exempt from withholding, but boot over $1,500 is subject to it, and if an exchange fails the QI generally withholds 3⅓% of the sale price.
Common mistakes: finding a QI after closing, letting proceeds land in your account, treating a personal second home as an investment, not lining up backups within 45 days, and forgetting FTB 3840 after leaving California.

Next step

When you decide to sell, bring in a CPA and a QI at the same time to confirm you qualify and what you would need to buy to defer the full gain. Tax decisions stay with your CPA.
