MLS and Online Exposure: Rules and Choices for Sellers

Most listings on home search sites start in the MLS. Knowing its rules helps you decide whether, when and how widely to go to market.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not legal advice. Your rights and duties depend on your contract and applicable law; please consult an attorney.

What the MLS is

A Multiple Listing Service is the system agents use to share listing information. Once a home is in the MLS, other agents can see it, and it usually flows through IDX and syndication to brokerage sites and public search sites. Different parts of the Bay Area may use different MLSs, so local rules control.

Key rules

As of November 2026, based on NAR's public statements:

  • Clear Cooperation: NAR's MLS policy requires a listing to be filed with the MLS within one business day of public marketing.
  • Delayed marketing: In March 2025, NAR introduced Multiple Listing Options for Sellers. A seller can put a listing in the MLS, visible to other participants, while holding off on IDX and syndication for a period set by each MLS.
  • Office exclusive: A separate exception in which the listing is marketed only within the brokerage.

The last two call for a signed seller disclosure confirming you understand the consequences.

Why this choice matters

The narrower the exposure, the fewer buyers see the home. Some sellers limit it for privacy, because of tenants, or because the home isn't ready. That can be reasonable, but it may cost you buyers and competition. Be clear about what you are getting in exchange.

Buyer-agent pay after the NAR settlement

Commissions are negotiable and there is no legal standard. The California DRE lists claims of a "standard" commission as a red flag. The 2024 settlement brought these changes:

  • Since August 17, 2024, offers of compensation to buyer agents can't appear on the MLS. Sellers can still offer compensation off the MLS, and can list concessions to buyers on it, such as help with closing costs.
  • Buyers working with an MLS participant must sign a written agreement before touring a home, stating the compensation in objective terms.
  • Under California's AB 2992, effective January 1, 2025, a buyer-broker agreement must be signed no later than the buyer's offer. It must state compensation, services, when payment is due and an expiration date no more than three months out.
  • A buyer may ask the seller to cover some or all of the buyer agent's compensation as a concession. The seller can accept, counter or decline.

That makes it a line item when you compare offers and estimate your net. See comparing offers. This is general information; your signed agreements control.

What to check once you're live

  • Address, size, lot and room count match across the MLS and public sites.
  • No obvious errors in public estimates or history.
  • Photos display in the right order with the right lead image.
  • Status stays current (Active, Pending, Sold).

Common mistakes

  • Promoting on social media before the listing is in the MLS, against the rules.
  • Assuming limited exposure is automatically more exclusive or better.
  • Treating page views as buyer demand.

Next step
