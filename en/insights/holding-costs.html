Holding Costs: Tax, Insurance, HOA, Mello-Roos and Repair Reserves

What a home really costs each month goes well past the mortgage payment. Here is each item, where to find the number, and how to build your own annual budget.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your situation; please consult a CPA or tax professional.

Why holding costs matter

Holding costs are what you pay simply for owning a home, on top of principal and interest. They shape what you can afford when buying, drive cash flow on a rental, and set how long you can carry an overlap during a move. Two homes with the same mortgage payment can cost very different amounts once HOA dues and Mello-Roos come into it.

Three items people underestimate

Mello-Roos

A Community Facilities District issues bonds for public facilities or services and repays them through a special tax on parcels inside it. Unlike the 1% base tax, the law does not set one allocation method, and many districts use measures such as square footage. The county collects it on your bill, and it runs until the district's obligations are met. On resales, the amount and remaining term are not always obvious. Sellers subject to such a lien generally must make a good-faith effort to obtain and deliver the district's notice, so read it when you buy.

HOA dues

Under Civil Code 5605, a board generally cannot raise regular assessments more than 20% over the prior year, or levy special assessments totaling more than 5% of budgeted gross expenses, without a member vote. Emergencies are handled separately. So a rise close to 20% is possible in a single year. Read the budget, reserve funding and minutes before you buy and while you own, and note any big-ticket items coming due. Ask your insurance professional about loss assessment coverage too.

Repair reserves

California has no official percentage. A more useful approach than any rule of thumb is to list the roof, HVAC, water heater, exterior paint, sewer line and appliances, record when each was last replaced, estimate remaining life and cost, and spread the total across the years. Condo owners usually leave the exterior to the HOA but still fund the interior. Our maintenance guide is a good place to start.

Tax limits to know

From IRS materials checked in November 2026: the 2026 cap on state and local tax deductions is $40,400 ($20,200 if married filing separately), reduced when modified AGI exceeds $505,000 and not below $10,000. Mortgage interest on loans taken out after December 15, 2017 is generally limited to $750,000 of debt. HOA fees are not deductible on Schedule A, and neither are water, sewer or trash charges. Flat charges on your tax bill are not all deductible property tax, so review each line. A CPA can tell you whether itemizing makes sense.

Common mistakes: budgeting from the previous owner's tax bill, since reassessment follows a sale; asking about dues but not special assessments; treating insurance as a fixed cost; and keeping no cash for repairs.

Next step

Put these items in a spreadsheet, divide by 12 and add the result to your mortgage payment. For a rental, also allow for vacancy and management costs, and ask a CPA which expenses you can deduct.
