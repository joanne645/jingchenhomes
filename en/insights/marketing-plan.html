Listing Marketing Plan: From Pricing to the First Week

A good listing plan starts with what buyers will be comparing and what can honestly be said about the home, and then works out when and how they will see it.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

What a marketing plan has to answer

Every listing plan should answer four questions: what are the likely buyers looking for, how does this home differ from the competition, what is the launch timing, and which signals will tell you to change course? Those decisions matter more than the number of channels.

Find selling points you can back up

Selling points should be specific and verifiable. "Roof replaced in 2023 with permit" says more than "well maintained." "South-facing primary bedroom with afternoon light" says more than "lots of natural light." List the confirmed facts, then lead with the three or four that matter most.

Leave out what you can't confirm. Give square footage with its source. Point buyers to the district to confirm schools by full address. Don't quote commute times; let buyers check for themselves.

Launch timing

  1. Preparation: repairs, cleaning, furnishing, photos, disclosures and records.
  2. A few days before: check photos, copy, square footage and lot data, and set showing rules.
  3. Launch week: the listing goes live on the MLS, with showings and possibly an open house. NAR suggests holding the first open house soon after listing, ideally that first weekend.
  4. Weeks two and three: review online views, showings and feedback to see whether the issue is price, condition or presentation.

Whether to set an offer date or use delayed marketing depends on the market and your goals. See the MLS and online exposure guide for the rules.

Rules for copy and ads

  • Under fair housing rules, ads describe the property, not the kind of person it suits or who lives nearby.
  • California law requires digitally altered listing images to be labeled, with the original available. See staging and photography.
  • Don't overstate price, size or condition, and avoid claims you can't support, such as "best in the area."

Reading the response

What you seePossible reason
Many online views, few showingsA gap between price and what the photos and copy show
Many showings, no offersCondition, layout or price doesn't match the visit
Few viewsCheck the price bracket, lead photo and address details

Treat this as a starting point. Read it with actual buyer feedback and any new competing listings.

Common mistakes

  • Going live before the home is ready and assuming you can fix it later.
  • Copy full of adjectives and short on facts.
  • Judging interest by friends' reactions and not by actual buyers.
  • Marketing and offer communication that don't match, so buyers hear different stories.
  • Changing several things at once, so you can't tell what made the difference.

Next step

Gather your confirmed facts, your records and your timeline, so the listing copy is ready to check before anything goes live. Exactly which marketing methods will be used, who handles them and what they cost should be settled in your listing discussion.
