What's My Home Worth? AVMs, Assessed Value, CMAs and Appraisals

Online estimates, your tax bill and an agent's number rarely match, and that is expected. They answer different questions, so it helps to know which one to use and when.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your situation; please consult a CPA or tax professional.

Why one home has several "values"

No official number tells you what your home is worth today. Pricing a sale, opening a HELOC, challenging your property tax and buying insurance all rely on different figures. Use the wrong one and you could misprice a listing or end up underinsured.

Where AVMs fall short

An automated valuation model estimates value from public records and sales data. Zillow itself says the Zestimate is not an appraisal and cannot replace one, calling it a free starting point. As of November 2026, it reports a nationwide median error of 7.21% for off-market homes. A median means about half of homes miss by more. On a $1.5 million house, 7% is roughly $105,000.

The blind spots are easy to picture. A model may not know whether the kitchen was redone, how the lot sits, whether the street is noisy, or whether an addition was permitted. Bay Area homes can vary widely within a single block. Averaging several sites does not fix this, because they often draw on similar data.

A federal quality control rule for AVMs took effect October 1, 2025, but it covers models used by mortgage originators and secondary market issuers. It does not mean the number on a consumer website has been validated by regulators.

Assessed value is not market value

Under Prop 13, assessed value generally rises no more than 2% a year, so long-held homes often sit well below market. That says nothing about what they would sell for. The opposite case is the useful one: if market value drops below assessed value, you can ask for a Prop 8 review. Alameda County accepts informal requests outside the formal appeal window, while Santa Clara County sets a deadline each summer.

CMAs and appraisals

A CMA compares your home with similar sold, pending and active listings nearby and adjusts for condition, and an agent can walk the property to catch what a model misses. Under California law, a licensee's opinion given in the course of their work may not be called an appraisal. When you need a formal valuation, for example for some loans, a divorce, or an estate or gift tax matter, use a licensed appraiser, and ask your attorney or CPA what is required.

If you would like to understand your home's market position, you can book a free home value consultation (a CMA discussion, not a formal appraisal). This article explains how to read one.

Keeping track over time

  1. Once a year, note recent sales of similar homes nearby. That tells you more than one online figure.
  2. Keep renovation invoices and permits, since AVMs cannot see them.
  3. When your assessment notice arrives around midsummer, compare it with the market and decide whether a review is worthwhile.
  4. Insure to rebuilding cost. Do not set coverage from market value or an AVM.
Common mistakes: deciding to sell or move based on one online number, negotiating from assessed value, and treating a lender's internal estimate as market value. We do not predict home prices.

Next step

Start with why you want the number, whether for a sale, a loan, a tax review or insurance, then choose the method that fits that purpose.
