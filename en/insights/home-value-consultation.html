Free Home Value Consultation: CMA vs. Appraisal

Most sellers start by asking what the house could sell for. It helps to know where a number came from and what it can be used for before you plan around it.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

A CMA and an appraisal are different things

A CMA (Comparative Market Analysis) is an agent's review of recent sales, active listings and details about your home. Its job is to frame a sensible range for a list price. It is not a valuation report.

An appraisal is a written professional opinion of value prepared by an appraiser. When a buyer finances a purchase, the lender usually needs one, and the borrower may be asked to pay for it. If you need a value for an estate, a divorce or a tax filing, ask your attorney or CPA what kind of report is required. A CMA is not a substitute.

Why the difference matters

One house can carry several numbers at once: a website estimate, a neighbor's sale price, an agent's CMA, a buyer's appraisal. They rest on different inputs, so a gap between them doesn't mean anyone is wrong. Knowing the source tells you which number belongs at which step.

Keep in mind that a buyer's appraisal can come in below the contract price. The buyer may then ask for a reduction, cover the difference, or use a contract right to cancel. Pricing from close comparable sales gives you something concrete to discuss if that happens.

What gets reviewed

  • Facts: address, property type, size, lot, age and layout.
  • Condition and changes: when the roof, HVAC, kitchen or baths were updated, and whether work was permitted.
  • Comparable sales: recent sales that are close in location, type, size and condition.
  • Competition: what is on the market now and will be compared with your home.
  • Your timing: when you need to move and whether you are also buying.

It helps to gather purchase documents, repair records, HOA materials and permit history ahead of time. The result is a price range and the reasoning behind it, not a price anyone can promise.

Using online estimates

Automated estimates come from public data and models. They can't see inside the house and may not know about a new roof. Treat one as a single data point, and don't read much into it whether it runs high or low.

Common mistakes

  • Fixing on the highest sale nearby and ignoring differences in size and condition.
  • Treating list prices as sale prices.
  • Adding renovation costs to the price. Spending isn't always recovered dollar for dollar.
  • Forgetting selling costs. The net proceeds calculator gives a rough first pass.
