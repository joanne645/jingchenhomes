Bay Area Home Buyer FAQ

Short answers to the eight questions buyers ask most, with links to more detail. Current as of November 2026.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not tax or legal advice. Please consult a CPA, tax professional or attorney about your situation.

Eight questions Bay Area buyers ask most often. Each one links to more detail where it helps.

1. Do I have to sign an agreement before seeing homes?

Not to walk into an open house on your own, where the agent acts for the seller. To have an agent show you homes, yes. The NAR settlement has required a written agreement before touring since August 17, 2024, and California DRE regulations require signing no later than your offer and presume it is practical to sign before a showing. Each term tops out at three months and cannot renew automatically.

2. Who pays the buyer's agent?

Commissions are negotiable and not set by law. Your agreement must state the amount or how it is calculated, and the agent can't collect more than that. You can ask the seller to pay in your offer, but the seller need not agree, which may leave the difference to you. Settle this before signing.

3. Do I need 20% down?

No. Twenty percent is a common reference point, and going below it usually means PMI. Programs like CalHFA have their own rules. In the Bay Area, also watch the 2026 conforming limit: $1,249,125 for a one-unit home in Santa Clara, Alameda, Contra Costa and San Mateo counties, per FHFA's November 25, 2025 announcement (more in our budget guide). The right amount depends on your reserves, so work it through with a loan professional.

4. Is there a three-day cancellation period?

Generally, no. The "three days" people mention comes from the TDS: if it's delivered after you sign an offer, the law gives you three days after in-person delivery or five days after mailing to terminate in writing. Otherwise your exits come from contract contingencies, like the C.A.R. RPA's common 17-day defaults (your contract controls) for investigation, loan and appraisal. The federal right to rescind mainly covers non-purchase loans such as refinances.

5. How much is the deposit, and can I lose it?

DRE's checklist says about 1%–3% of the price, and the RPA default has it in escrow within three business days of acceptance. Cancelling under a live contingency normally brings it back. Walking away without a contractual right after removing contingencies can expose it to the liquidated damages clause. Ask an attorney about your specific situation.

6. The seller already inspected. Do I need my own?

Read the existing reports, then decide. They were ordered by the seller, and their date and scope may not suit you. The CFPB points out that an inspector works for you and suggests inspecting before you are finally committed. See the inspection guide.

7. What will my property tax be?

The base is 1% of taxable value, plus local bonds and special taxes, so it varies by home. Under Prop 13, taxable value rises at most 2% a year, but a sale resets it to market value. A supplemental bill usually follows for the difference through that fiscal year. It goes to you directly, and lender impound accounts generally don't pay it. Santa Clara and Alameda counties have estimators. Owner-occupants can also claim the $7,000 Homeowners' Exemption.

8. How do I confirm which school an address maps to?

Use the district's official tool with the full address, and ask the district about space for that year. A listing is no basis, and even a locator result doesn't guarantee a seat. See how to look it up.

This is general information as of November 2026. Your documents control, and contract, tax and loan questions belong with an attorney, CPA or loan professional.
