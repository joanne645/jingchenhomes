Seller Escrow and Closing: From Acceptance to Proceeds

Escrow is the neutral process that holds money and documents until the contract terms are met. Sellers have plenty to do, and knowing the order helps.

Reviewed Nov 5, 2026 · By Jing Chen

General information only, not tax or legal advice. Please consult a CPA, tax professional or attorney about your situation.

The usual sequence

  1. Open escrow: after signing, the buyer's deposit goes into escrow and the title company prepares a preliminary report.
  2. Disclosures: the seller delivers the TDS, Seller Property Questionnaire, natural hazard report and any HOA documents on the contract's schedule.
  3. Buyer investigation: inspections, permit checks, then possibly a request for repairs or a credit. You can agree, counter or decline.
  4. Contingency removal: the buyer removes contingencies under the contract. The periods are those in your signed agreement.
  5. Appraisal and loan: if the buyer is financing, the lender handles appraisal and underwriting.
  6. Payoff and settlement: escrow requests your loan payoff and prepares the settlement statement.
  7. Signing and recording: you sign the grant deed and other documents, usually notarized. Title transfers when the deed records.
  8. Possession and proceeds: you hand over keys as agreed and escrow pays out your net.

The money

A settlement statement usually covers your loan balance, agreed brokerage compensation (negotiable, with no legal standard), transfer tax, title and escrow charges, property tax proration, HOA charges, and agreed credits or repairs.

Transfer tax (as of November 2026): the county rate is $1.10 per $1,000. Some cities add their own:

Palo Alto$3.30 per $1,000
Mountain View$3.30 per $1,000; $15 when the total price exceeds $6 million (Measure G). Confirm how it applies with title

Fremont, Dublin, Pleasanton, San RamonNo additional city tax listed by county sources

Records for Sunnyvale and Cupertino show the city simply shares the county tax. Milpitas could not be verified from an official source. Confirm all of these with your title company. A January 2026 title-company guide says the county tax is customarily paid by sellers in Santa Clara, Alameda and Contra Costa, with city taxes commonly split, but all of it is negotiable and your contract controls. The net proceeds calculator gives an estimate.

Tax withholding and reporting

  • California withholding: per the FTB's 2026 Form 593 instructions, when the price is over $100,000, escrow generally withholds 3⅓% of the price and sends it to the FTB, unless you certify an exemption on Form 593, such as principal residence or a loss. You may instead elect a calculation based on estimated gain. This is a prepayment settled on your return, not the final tax.
  • FIRPTA: per the IRS, when the seller is a foreign person, the buyer generally must withhold 15% of the amount realized, with exceptions for certain residences at lower prices. U.S. sellers usually certify non-foreign status, for example with a W-9, and escrow will ask.
  • Reporting: if you receive Form 1099-S, the IRS says you must report the sale even when all the gain is excludable. The §121 exclusion is covered in the seller FAQ.

This is general information. Ask a CPA about your amounts and eligibility.

Common mistakes

  • Finding liens, a HELOC or a name problem on title days before closing.
  • Not allowing extra time for trusts, estates or signing from overseas.
  • Following an email with "new wire instructions." Always call escrow at a verified number.
  • Leaving belongings behind, or taking items you agreed to leave.
  • Canceling homeowners insurance before the deed records.

Next step

Once the contract is signed, put every date on a calendar. Raise document and tax questions early with your escrow officer and CPA.
