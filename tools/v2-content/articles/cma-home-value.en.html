What a CMA is

A Comparative Market Analysis uses nearby similar listings and sales to work out where a home sits in the market. An agent usually prepares it. It informs pricing and offers, and it is not a formal appraisal.

An appraisal comes from an appraiser working for the lender, and the CFPB notes you are entitled to a copy before closing. Portal numbers are something else again: Zillow states that the Zestimate is not an appraisal and can't be used in place of one.

Why buyers need one

A list price is a seller's strategy. It may be set low to draw multiple offers or set high to leave room. A CMA helps you answer three questions: Is this range reasonable? What did similar homes actually sell for? Could the appraisal come in short? Those answers tell you how far you are willing to go and whether to keep an appraisal contingency.

How to build one

  1. Choose comparables. Same property type, close by, similar size, bedrooms and lot, in the same school boundary or street setting when possible, and as recent as you can get.
  2. Look at three groups. Sold homes give real prices. Pending homes show the latest direction, though the final price is unknown. Active listings are your competition.
  3. Adjust item by item. Condition, parking, lot shape, noise, views, freeway exposure.
  4. Check sale terms. Seller credits, family transfers or distressed sales can distort a price.
  5. Finish with a range, not a single figure, then decide where your offer sits.

Common mistakes

  • Applying one price per square foot. Smaller homes tend to run higher per foot, and lots and condition spread it further.
  • Using the wrong square footage. Listing and county figures can differ. Be careful with unpermitted additions.
  • Crossing a boundary. A different school district or noise exposure across one road can make a home a poor comparable.
  • Using stale sales. When the market moves, even a sale from a few months ago needs adjusting.
  • Ignoring repairs. Roof or sewer work found in inspection belongs in your total cost, based on quotes.
  • Reading analysis as a forecast. A CMA summarizes current evidence, not future prices.

Look at carrying costs too

Two homes at similar prices can differ on tax bill add-ons, HOA dues and insurance, and sometimes that matters more than the price gap. Run both through the buyer cost calculator.

A CMA is market analysis, not an appraisal by a licensed appraiser, and it guarantees no price. Loan appraisals come from an appraiser engaged through the lender.
