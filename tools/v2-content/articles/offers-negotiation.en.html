Buyer-agent compensation requests

Commissions are negotiable and there is no legal standard. After the 2024 NAR settlement, buyer-agent pay is agreed between the buyer and their agent. The California DRE explains that a buyer may ask the seller to cover some or all of it as a concession, and the seller can accept or reject. Any amount you accept belongs in your net calculation.

Common negotiating moves

  • Counteroffer: respond on price or terms. A counter generally ends the original offer, so weigh that first.
  • Multiple offers: you might ask for highest and best, or counter more than one buyer. If you do the latter, the wording must prevent two acceptances.
  • Change only what matters: focus on the one or two terms that count.
  • Backup offer: a fallback if the first buyer drops out.

Contingencies and deposits

Until contingencies are removed, a buyer can usually exit under the contract. The periods are whatever your signed contract says, so track each date. If one passes, follow the contract, for example by issuing a notice to perform.

Under Civil Code §1675, when a buyer intends to occupy a home of up to four units and the contract has a liquidated damages clause, a deposit of 3% of the price or less is generally valid, and a larger amount has to be shown reasonable. Talk to an attorney if a default occurs.

If a disclosure, or a material amendment, is delivered after the buyer signs, Civil Code §1102.3 gives the buyer time to terminate: three days after personal delivery, five after mailing or electronic delivery. That is one more reason to have disclosures ready early.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at the top price and ignoring financing and appraisal risk.
  • Deciding on the basis of a buyer's personal letter. Details about family, ethnicity or religion raise fair housing concerns, so compare terms only.
  • Relying on verbal agreements. California real estate contracts need to be in signed writing.
  • Treating a pre-approval as an approved loan.
  • Letting a deadline push you into signing before reading everything.
