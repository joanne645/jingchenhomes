What escrow is

Escrow is a neutral third party holding documents and funds under both sides' written instructions until the conditions are met. DRE calls it a clearing house for transferring real estate. It doesn't represent either side and won't tell you whether your contract is a good one.

Independent escrow companies are licensed by the DFPI. Title companies, attorneys and brokers that handle escrow answer to their own regulators. You can check discipline records on those agencies' sites before choosing.

Federal timing rules

  • Loan Estimate: within three business days of the lender receiving your application.
  • Closing Disclosure: you must receive it at least three business days before consummation, usually when you sign the loan documents.
  • What restarts the three days: an APR increase of more than 1/8 of a point on a fixed-rate loan or 1/4 of a point on an adjustable-rate loan, a new prepayment penalty, or a change of loan product. Typos, walk-through issues and most changes to payments at closing don't.

The CFPB opened a request for information on TRID timing in July 2026. As of November 2026 we found no change to the rule, so go by what your lender tells you.

Reading the Closing Disclosure

  1. Compare the rate, fees and payment line by line with your last Loan Estimate.
  2. Check names, how title is held, and that your deposit and credits appear.
  3. Confirm Cash to Close. Take questions to your lender or settlement agent early.
  4. Ask for the full loan document set in advance, not at the signing table.

How you take title can affect inheritance and taxes. Ask an attorney or CPA, since escrow and agents can't decide this for you.

Wire safety

Closing funds are a favorite target for scammers. The CFPB advises choosing two trusted contacts early and noting their direct numbers, confirming account details by phone on a number you already have, and never following instructions in an email. If a wire goes wrong, call your bank at once to request a recall and report it to the FBI's IC3.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving insurance until the end. Your lender needs proof of coverage.
  • Changing finances near closing. A new job or large transfer can delay funding.
  • Expecting escrow to track your deadlines. Those are for you and your agent to manage.
  • Forgetting taxes afterward. Supplemental bills go to the owner, not through the lender's impound account. Alameda County says they mail year-round, most within 12 months. If the home is your residence, file the Homeowners' Exemption (BOE-266) with the county assessor by February 15 for the full exemption, a $7,000 reduction in taxable value.
This is general information, not legal, tax or loan advice. Your contract and lender set the actual timing. Take tax and vesting questions to a CPA or attorney.
