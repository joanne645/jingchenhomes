What's the commission? Do I have to pay the buyer's agent?

Commissions are negotiable and there is no legal standard. The California DRE says commissions are fully negotiable under state law and treats claims of a "standard" rate as a red flag. Since August 17, 2024, offers of compensation to buyer agents haven't appeared on the MLS, and buyer-agent pay is agreed between buyers and their agents. A buyer can ask you in an offer to cover some or all of it, and you can accept or decline. Count it in your net. See MLS and online exposure.

Do I need to renovate first?

Not necessarily. Start with cleaning, decluttering and anything that affects function or safety, then weigh larger projects one at a time. No project is guaranteed to pay for itself. See prep and repairs.

Can I sell as-is and skip disclosures?

As-is generally means you aren't agreeing to make repairs. It doesn't remove disclosure duties. Sellers of one-to-four-unit homes generally provide a TDS and other required disclosures, with limited exceptions. If a disclosure comes late or is materially amended, Civil Code §1102.3 lets the buyer terminate within three days (personal delivery) or five (mail or electronic). Ask an attorney about your situation.

How much tax will I owe on my home?

Per the IRS, as of November 2026, eligible sellers can exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 on a joint return. You generally need to have owned and lived in the home for two of the five years before the sale and not have used the exclusion in the prior two years. Gain above that is taxable. Gain is the sale price less your adjusted basis and selling costs, so keep your purchase and improvement records. Work, health or unforeseen events may allow a partial exclusion. Depreciation from rental use can't be excluded. Have a CPA review the California side too.

What is California's 3⅓% withholding?

Per the FTB's 2026 Form 593 instructions, when the price is over $100,000, escrow generally withholds 3⅓% of the price and sends it to the FTB. It's a prepayment settled on your return. Exemptions include a home used as a principal residence for two of the last five years, a home last used as a principal residence, or a sale with no gain. You may elect a calculation based on estimated gain instead. False certifications carry penalties.

I'm not a U.S. tax resident. What changes?

FIRPTA applies when the seller is a foreign person. Per the IRS, the buyer generally withholds 15% of the amount realized. There are exceptions for individuals buying a residence: generally no withholding at $300,000 or less and 10% at $1 million or less, if the buyer meets the residency requirement. A withholding certificate (Form 8288-B) can reduce withholding but takes time, so see a CPA early. U.S. sellers can certify non-foreign status, for example with a W-9.

Should I sell first or buy first?

It depends on financing, transition costs and the home you want. Selling first makes your funds clear but may mean a temporary move. Buying first avoids moving twice but can mean carrying two payments or bridge financing. A rent-back is one cushion, if a buyer agrees. Run the numbers with a loan professional.

How long from listing to money in hand?

There's no fixed timeline. It depends on preparation, the market and your contract, and escrow length is whatever the parties agree. Leave some slack, especially if you are also buying or moving out of state. The net proceeds calculator can estimate your proceeds, and seller escrow and closing covers the steps.
