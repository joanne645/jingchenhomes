Why moving up is tricky

Most of your money sits in your current home, yet the down payment on the next one is often needed before that home closes. Get the order wrong and you may end up in a short-term rental or carrying two loans. Before touring, work out what you might net from the sale, how much cash the next home needs, and how long an overlap you could handle. The net proceeds calculator and buyer costs calculator give a starting estimate.

Three paths

PathUpsideTrade-off
Sell firstFunds are known, and no double housing costsYou may need interim housing or a short rent-back from the buyer
Buy firstAn unhurried move, and your purchase is not tied to a saleRequires enough cash or credit, plus payments and costs during the overlap
Contingent offerYour purchase depends on selling, so you can exit if it does not sellSellers may refuse or keep marketing the home, and it is a weaker offer when competition is strong

No path is best for everyone. It depends on how saleable your home is, your available cash and your household's timing. The contract sets out any contingency's deadlines and removal terms, so read them closely.

Bridging the money

  • HELOC: a revolving line against your equity. The CFPB notes these usually have variable rates, that a lender may limit or freeze the line if your home's value falls significantly or your ability to repay is in doubt, and that you could lose your home if you cannot repay. If you plan to use one, apply before listing, since approval may be harder afterward.
  • Bridge loan: short-term financing that draws on your current home's equity. Rates, fees and terms vary widely by lender, so we do not quote figures or recommend lenders. Ask what happens if the home has not sold at maturity.
  • Qualifying with two payments: your existing mortgage generally still counts when you apply for a new loan. Have a loan professional review this early.

Prop 19 base year transfers

Owners who are 55 or older, severely disabled or disaster victims can move the taxable value of their main home to a replacement bought or built within two years, anywhere in California. Those 55 or older or disabled can do this up to three times (as of November 2026). The value test depends on timing:

Replacement bought"Equal or lesser value" limit
Before selling the original100% of the original's market value
First year after the sale105%
Second year after the sale110%

Any amount above the limit is added to the transferred value. In the BOE's example, an original home worth $400,000 with a $100,000 taxable value, replaced for $600,000 in the first year, has a $420,000 limit. The $180,000 excess produces a $280,000 taxable value.

If you buy first, you pay tax on the new home's full market value until the original sells, and that amount is not refunded. File BOE-19-B with the assessor in the new home's county after both transactions close and you have moved in, within three years of buying. It is not handled through escrow.

Tax and other pitfalls

  • You can generally exclude $250,000 of gain on a main home, or $500,000 on a joint return, if you owned and lived there two of the last five years. Ask a CPA about any excess.
  • Expect a possible supplemental tax bill on the new home, and file the Homeowners' Exemption there.
  • Put any rent-back in writing, including insurance, deposit and holdover terms.
