Larger projectsKitchen, bath, roof, structural workWeigh time, cost and risk one at a time; often optional

Three questions for each item

  1. Would this make buyers offer less, ask for repairs, or skip the house?
  2. What will it cost and how long will it take? Will it push back your listing date?
  3. If you don't do it, can price or a credit handle it instead?

No renovation is guaranteed to pay for itself. Whether to start depends on the house and on what comparable homes offer at your price.

Pre-listing inspections

Some sellers order a home or pest inspection before listing so that problems don't surprise them during escrow. It also helps you price the home and answer buyer questions with more confidence. Keep in mind that once you know about a material issue, you generally have to disclose it, so think that through before you order a report. Cost and scope are between you and the inspector.

Hiring contractors in California

As of November 2026, according to the Contractors State License Board:

  • Since January 1, 2025, unlicensed work is limited to jobs under $1,000 in total (labor, materials and everything else), with no building permit required and no hired workers. Anything beyond that needs a licensed contractor.
  • Home improvement projects over $500 need a written contract.
  • The down payment can't exceed $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less.

Check the license on the CSLB website before work starts. Pull permits when they are required, even if you are in a hurry. Unpermitted work tends to surface when buyers investigate, and it has to be disclosed.

Repairs don't replace disclosure

Sellers of one-to-four-unit homes in California generally provide a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS). A completed repair doesn't erase what you know: past leaks or insurance claims may still need to be disclosed. Keep invoices, warranties and permits together. For what must be disclosed in your case, consult an attorney.

Common mistakes

  • Finding out late that the work will run weeks past the listing date.
  • Choosing finishes for personal taste.
  • Covering a problem cosmetically and not disclosing it.
  • Small jobs that add up to more than the budget.
  • Paying too much up front, or hiring without checking the license.
