1. Does the Homeowners' Exemption start automatically?

No. File form BOE-266 with the county assessor. You file once, and it continues while the home stays your main residence. It takes $7,000 off assessed value as of November 2026, or about $70 a year at 1%. To get the full year's benefit, file by February 15. Tell the assessor when you stop qualifying; December 10 is the last day to end it without penalty. See property tax and insurance.

2. I just closed and got a second tax bill. What is it?

It is probably supplemental tax. After a sale or completed construction, the county bills the difference between the old and new value, prorated for the rest of the fiscal year. Events between January 1 and May 31 produce two supplemental assessments. These arrive separately from your annual bill, so confirm the sender is the county tax collector and ask whether your impound account pays them. If you disagree with the value, an appeal is generally due within 60 days of the notice, with exceptions in some counties.

3. Prices fell. Can my property tax go down?

Possibly. If market value is below assessed value, you can ask for a Prop 8 decline-in-value review. The reduction is temporary, and the value can climb back as the market recovers. Deadlines vary: formal 2026 appeals closed September 15 in Alameda and Santa Clara and run through November 30 in Contra Costa. Check your county's site each year.

4. Will a remodel raise my property tax?

It depends on the work. The BOE says normal maintenance and repairs typically are not treated as new construction. Additions are assessed, but only the new portion, and your existing value stays put. A major rehabilitation can also count. The increase reflects the market value added, which is not necessarily what you spent. Unpermitted work still has to be disclosed when you sell. See maintenance and renovation.

5. Does a handyman need a license for small jobs?

As of November 2026, the Contractors State License Board (CSLB) requires a license for work of $1,000 or more in combined labor and materials, and for any job needing a permit or involving workers. Look it up at cslb.ca.gov. Down payments are limited to 10% or $1,000, whichever is less. Breaking a job into smaller invoices is a poor workaround and leaves you with little recourse if something goes wrong.

6. Can I list my ADU on a short-term rental site?

JADUs cannot be short-term rentals, and ADUs approved under Government Code section 66323 must be rented for more than 30 days, per HCD's March 2026 handbook. For other ADUs, your city's rules decide. Many cities restrict short-term rentals, so ask the city and check any HOA rules first. See ADU basics.

7. How much can I raise the rent this year?

For units under AB 1482, increases starting between August 1, 2026 and July 31, 2027 are capped at 8.8% in Alameda and Contra Costa counties and 8.6% in Santa Clara and other counties, per the Attorney General's site as of November 2026. Many individually owned single-family homes and condos are exempt if the required written notice was given. San Jose and Mountain View set lower local limits for certain older apartments. See rental management.

8. How much gain can I exclude when I sell? Does renting it out matter?

If you owned and lived in the home for two of the last five years, you can generally exclude $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 on a joint return, for both federal and California purposes. A former rental may still qualify, but gain equal to depreciation cannot be excluded, and periods of nonqualified use after 2008 can reduce the exclusion. Pure investment property follows different rules, covered in selling an investment property. Ask a CPA to run your numbers.
